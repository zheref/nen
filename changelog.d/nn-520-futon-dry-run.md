- `nen parse futon` reads a typed `--dry-run` as a flag of the scope, as it reads `--idle` (zheref/nen#520). It is the first or the last token before any `then`, or its own argv word, and both flags may be typed together in either order. `--json` gains `dryRun` (`true` when typed, otherwise `false`); the plain rendering adds `dry-run: yes (...)`. nen reports the flag and never acts on it: what a dry futon run does is the calling skill's rule.
- Behaviour changes: an UNQUOTED label whose first or last word is `--dry-run` now reads as the flag, where `nen@bug --dry-run` used to parse at exit 0 as the label `bug --dry-run`: quote the label to keep it. `--dry-run` after `then`, or typed twice, exits 2 with the corrected line. Typed twice as argv words (`nen parse futon --dry-run nen@bug --dry-run`), either flag is now refused at exit 2 where `--idle` used to collapse to one and exit 0: the argv reader counts a repeated boolean (`ParsedArgs.booleanCounts`) while `booleans` still collapses it for every other verb. A caller comparing the `--json` report by exact equality must add `dryRun`. `nen parse <skill> --dry-run` on any skill but futon exits 2.
- `nen parse futon` refuses at exit 2 any other flag-shaped word in the scope rather than reading it into the label: a near miss of `--idle` or `--dry-run` (`--dryrun`, `--DRY-RUN`, `--dry_run`, `-dry-run`, an em dash, `--dry-run=false`), with the exact flag in the corrected line; an unknown `--option`; and a scope flag left inside an unquoted label (`nen@x --dry-run y`, which used to parse as the label `x --dry-run y`). A flag after `then` is caught when punctuation follows it and ignored inside quotes. Text after a bare `--` is refused rather than dropped. The plain rendering strips control characters from the echoed line, label, `then` clause and `try:` line; `--json` is unchanged. Behaviour change: an unquoted label carrying any of those words now exits 2: quote it to keep it.
